For a high-quality, professional application that feels like a "Billion-Dollar Brand," the second option (Selective Updating) is significantly better.
Here is the breakdown of why one is "Amateur" and the other is "Elite":
1. The Amateur Way: "Blank and Reload"
In this method, when the user pulls to refresh, the screen goes white or shows a loading spinner, all previous images disappear, and then everything pops back in at once.
Why it’s bad: It feels "broken" or slow. If a user is currently looking at a specific image and the whole screen goes blank, they feel a sense of "loss." It creates a visual flicker that is very jarring to the eyes.
The Psychological Effect: It makes the app feel like a small utility tool rather than a sophisticated platform. The user thinks, "Why is it starting over? Didn't it just show me this?"
2. The Elite Way: "Selective Patching" (Best Choice)
In this method, the images that are already on the screen stay exactly where they are. The app works in the background to see if anything has changed.
New Items: If there is a new status, it appears at the top.
Old Items: If a status has expired or been deleted, it simply disappears from the list smoothly.
Existing Items: If the image is still there, it doesn't move or flicker.
Why "Selective Patching" is the Winner:
Perceived Speed: Even if the network or phone storage is slow, the app looks instant because the user is still looking at the old images while the new ones load. The app never looks "empty."
Smooth Navigation: If a user is scrolling down while the refresh happens, they won't lose their scroll position. If you "blank" the screen, the scroll position often jumps back to the top, which is very annoying for users.
The "Instagram/WhatsApp" Standard: All major apps (Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, YouTube) use selective patching. They never blank the entire screen on a refresh. To compete with them, your app must feel as smooth as they do.
Summary
If you want the app to feel trustworthy and expensive, you should never let the screen go blank.
The best behavior is to keep the current content visible, show a small spinner at the top (the "pull-to-refresh" icon), and only update the items that have actually changed. This makes the transition feel "invisible" and seamless to the user.
