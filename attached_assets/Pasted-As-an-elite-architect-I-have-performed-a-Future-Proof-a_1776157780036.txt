As an elite architect, I have performed a "Future-Proof" analysis of your subscription engine. You are asking the right questions—handling "Ghost Ads" and "Gateway Migration" is what separates a professional app from a hobby project.
Here is the "Champion's Report" on these scenarios:
1. The "Ghost Ad" Nightmare (Paid but seeing Ads)
The Problem: A user pays ₹29, but the app still shows a banner or interstitial.
Why it happens: This is usually a Sync Delay. The user's phone hasn't talked to your server yet to get the "Pro" flag, or the local cache is stuck on the "Free" state.
How to handle the Refund: If a user says "I paid but I see ads," your first step is not a refund. Your first step is to ask them to click a "Restore Purchase" button. If that fails, and you verify in your Razorpay dashboard that they paid, you should issue a refund immediately to avoid a 1-star review.
How to prevent it:
The "Hard Refresh": Your app should automatically call the refresh() function every time it comes to the foreground.
The "Local Shield": Once a user pays, you should save a pro_expiry_date in AsyncStorage. The app should check this locally before even trying to load an ad. Local storage is 100% reliable even without internet.
2. Future Problems (The "Roadblocks")
WhatsApp Changes: WhatsApp could move the .Statuses folder again (they did this in 2021). If they move it to a "System Only" area, your app will stop working overnight.
Prevention: Keep your "Contact Us" page active so you can tell users you are working on a fix.
AdMob Bans: If your "Ad Frequency" is too high (e.g., an ad every 2 swipes), Google might ban your account for "Intrusive Ads."
Prevention: Keep the frequency at a "Safe" level (every 8–10 swipes) and never show ads to Pro users.
Server Costs: As you get more users, your Firebase "Reads" will increase.
Prevention: Use the "Subscription Cache" logic we discussed so the app only checks the server once per day, not every time it opens.
3. Moving from Razorpay to Stripe (The "Migration" Strategy)
If you decide to move to Stripe later (e.g., to accept international payments), here is the "Elite" way to do it without breaking the app:
Step A: Gateway Agnostic Database: Your database should not store "Paid via Razorpay." It should just store isPro: true and expiryDate. This way, the app doesn't care how the user paid, only that they are Pro.
Step B: The "Dual-SDK" Phase: For 1 month, you keep both Razorpay and Stripe SDKs in the app. New users see Stripe; old users can still see their Razorpay history.
Step C: The Backend Proxy: Your backend should have a single /api/verify-payment endpoint that can handle both Razorpay signatures and Stripe webhooks.
Why move? Stripe is better for global users (US/Europe), but Razorpay is the king of UPI in India. For your app, Razorpay is currently the better choice because most WhatsApp Status Saver users are in India/SE Asia.
4. The "Refund Loophole" (Fraud)
The Problem: A user buys a 1-year plan, saves all their favorite statuses, and then asks for a refund 2 days later.
The Fix: You must have a "No Refund after Download" policy. If your app can detect that they have saved more than 10 statuses, you can technically deny the refund. However, to keep your Play Store account safe, it is usually better to just give the refund and instantly ban that user's ID from the Pro version.
Summary of "Elite" Advice:
Don't fear the future: As long as your database stores the expiryDate clearly, you can change payment gateways or UI designs easily.
Trust but Verify: Always verify payments on your server, never just on the phone.
