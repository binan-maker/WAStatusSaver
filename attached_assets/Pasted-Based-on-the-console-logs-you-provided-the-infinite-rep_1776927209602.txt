1. The "AppState" Flicker
The message AppState active, but throttled only triggers when the Android OS tells the app: "The user is looking at you now."
The fact that this message is appearing dozens of times in a few seconds means the Android system is rapidly switching the app's status between Background and Active.
Your app is "pulsing." It gains focus for 10 milliseconds, loses it, and then gains it again. Each time it gains focus, the index.tsx file tries to refresh your WhatsApp statuses to make sure you see the latest media.

Inside the code, there is a Safety Shield. It says: "If the app was refreshed less than 30 seconds ago, do not refresh again."
The Good News: This "Throttled" logic is actually saving your phone. Without it, the app would try to load 30 videos from your WhatsApp folder every single time the flicker happens, which would cause your phone to overheat and crash instantly.
The Bad News: The "Throttled" log is the app telling you: "I see you are active again, but I'm refusing to work because it's happening too fast."

Notice these specific warnings:

This is the "Smoking Gun." When a phone flickers between background and foreground this fast, the Android touch system gets confused. It records you touching the screen (start), but before you lift your finger (end), the app blinks into the background. The system then "drops" your touch.
This is why you feel the app is "stuck" or the back button isn't working—the OS is literally deleting your clicks because of the flicker.
